## 1. Do the major platforms include WH-question or "who" (people-recognition) training, and how (real photos vs cartoons, personalised family photos, voice prompts, speech recognition)?

### Takeaway
Explicit WH-question content turned up in only one of the big platforms: CARD's Camp Discovery, which has a "Wh-Discrimination" lesson and a "Community Helpers" lesson. It uses generic stimuli ("illustrations or photographs, depending on the lesson"), not the family's own photos, and it appears poorly maintained. The large learning apps (Otsimo, Speech Blubs, Lingokids) do not advertise WH or "who" training. AAC apps (Proloquo2Go, TouchChat, Avaz, Jellow and others) let you put your own photos on buttons. They are expressive communication boards, though, not receptive "Who is this? Tap the right person" quiz trainers. No major platform found offers a structured, receptive "who" programme built on the family's own photos with a 2-choice field and spoken prompts.

### Cited Findings
**Camp Discovery (CARD) — the only major platform with explicit WH content**
- The App Store lesson list includes "Wh-Discrimination" and "Community Helpers", alongside Objects, Body Parts, Emotions, Actions, Gender, Functions, Categories, Describe and others. No "people/family" lesson is listed, and the listing does not say whether it uses real photos or illustrations. — [App Store: Camp Discovery-Autism](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1521799184)
- The peer-reviewed RCT says the app's stimuli "included either illustrations or photographs, depending on the lesson". Skills were receptive identification of single labels, such as picking "happy" or "doctor" from a field of stimuli, using a modified discrete-trial teaching (DTT) format. — [Novack et al., Behavior Analysis in Practice (PMC6411554)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6411554)
- It is built on CARD's ABA curriculum, and preference assessments and mini-games serve as rewards. — [Easter Seals Tech: Camp Discovery](https://eastersealstech.com/2015/09/30/camp-discovery-autism-learning-games)

**Otsimo (Turkey)**

**Video modelling platforms**
- Gemiini is a video-modelling subscription. Video modelling as a technique is rated "Established" by the National Autism Center (as summarised by NeuroLaunch). — [NeuroLaunch: Gemiini review (Aug 2026)](https://neurolaunch.com/gemiini-review/)

### Inferences
- The "who" concept is served today mainly by generic WH apps (another researcher's scope) and by DIY tools such as ABA Cards, or by AAC apps used for something they weren't designed for. None of the big platforms combines a receptive "Who is ___?" prompt, the family's own photos, a parent-recorded or TTS question and a small (2-choice) field.
- Speech recognition in big apps (Otsimo Speech, Speech Blubs) targets expressive imitation and articulation, not WH comprehension. Reviews suggest the recognition can be lenient or inaccurate.

## 2. Platform profiles: developer and country, platforms, pricing and business model, target users, evidence claims (and peer review), ratings, praise and complaints

### Takeaway
There are two business models. Learning and speech apps (Otsimo, Speech Blubs, Leeloo, Lingokids, Gemiini) are freemium subscriptions at roughly $60–$165 a year, or $98 a month for Gemiini, with "lifetime" upsells of $149–$230. Robust AAC apps (Proloquo2Go, TouchChat, LAMP WFL, Avaz Lifetime) are high one-time purchases of $150–$300 in the US, plus paid vocabulary add-ons, and TD Snap is shifting to subscription speech unlocks. Peer-reviewed efficacy evidence is thin. Camp Discovery has one small RCT run by developer-affiliated authors. Speech Blubs cites a therapist quality rating and a trial "in progress". MITA's evidence is observational and preprint, with company authors.

#### Autism Learning Games: Camp Discovery (Center for Autism and Related Disorders, US)
- Free, with a "Pro" version. US App Store: 2.4★ from 8 ratings, English only, last version 2.0.2 (11 May 2023). Reviews report sound stopping (2024), "Destination host has an erroneous SSL certificate" and only 2 modules accessible (Oct 2025), and Pro disappearing (2021). — [App Store: Camp Discovery](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1521799184)
- Peer-reviewed RCT (Novack, Hong, Dixon & Granpeesheh, *Behavior Analysis in Practice*, 2018/2019): 28 verbal autistic children aged about 2.5–9. The immediate group mastered 58.1 targets vs 8.4 for the delayed controls (p<.001, d=2.33), at about 4.5 targets per hour, and skills were maintained. Limitations: small sample, a research assistant present at every session, generalisation not tested, no fidelity data, and authors employed by CARD (a conflict of interest). — [PMC6411554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6411554)
- A researcher quoted in the press says it "isn't a replacement for actual therapeutic intervention". — [Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/news/camp-discovery-uses-ipad-teach-kids-autism)

## 5. Parent sentiment: cost complaints, subscription fatigue, screen time, generalisation, engagement

### Takeaway
Reddit could not be accessed, so this section relies on app-store reviews, Common Sense Media and parent blogs. The recurring themes are: (1) high cost and aggressive subscription mechanics (trial-to-paid charges, hard-to-cancel plans, per-device lifetime fees, upsell prompts even when subscribed); (2) repetitive content and slow content updates; (3) bugs, sync and login failures, and abandoned apps; (4) strong engagement anecdotes ("the only app that gets my non verbal son to play appropriately"). Researchers and advocates warn that apps are not a replacement for therapy and that generalisation to real life is untested.

### Cited Findings

Therapy is described as "prohibitively expensive or difficult to schedule" for many families. — [Columbia Magazine (Fall 2025)](https://magazine.columbia.edu/article/can-ai-fill-gap-childhood-speech-therapy)

### Inferences
- Subscription fatigue is real and easy to exploit as a differentiator. A one-time or free personal app with no account, no upsells and no "lifetime per device" fees answers the top complaint category directly.
- Reviews of stale, buggy autism apps show that reliability (offline, no server dependency) matters as much as features for this audience.
- "Needs an adult" is a complaint for generic apps but a design feature for a parent-co-play "who" app. Framing it as joint attention or parent modelling time may also defuse screen-time worries.

### Gaps
- No direct Reddit (r/autismparenting, r/autism, r/slp) or Facebook-group evidence could be gathered: the search tool blocks reddit.com and Facebook groups are private. The report writer should not attribute specific Reddit sentiment to these notes.
- No quantitative survey of parent app-spend or screen-time attitudes specific to autism apps was found.

## 6. Gaps a small, personalised "who"-question app could fill

### Takeaway
Six things are missing from the major platforms. (1) A receptive "Who is this? / Who is eating?" trainer built on the family's own photos. (2) Parent-recorded prompts in the home language, especially Indian languages such as Marathi or Hindi. (3) An errorless-friendly 2-choice field. (4) Offline, account-free privacy. (5) No subscription. (6) Active maintenance. Incumbents cover pieces of this: Camp Discovery has WH and community-helper DTT with generic images, English only, and is poorly maintained; AAC apps support own photos and recordings but no quiz; Avaz and Jellow cover Indian languages but have no WH training.

### Cited Findings
- Generic WH content exists only in Camp Discovery's "Wh-Discrimination" (English only, last updated May 2023, 2.4★ from 8 ratings, SSL errors in 2025). — [App Store: Camp Discovery](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1521799184)
- Its stimuli are generic illustrations or photos, not the family's own. — [PMC6411554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6411554)
- Otsimo and Speech Blubs listings do not mention WH questions or family photos. — [App Store: Otsimo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/otsimo-special-education-1/id1084723774); [App Store: Speech Blubs](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1239522573)
- Own-photo and recording support exists, but only inside AAC communication boards (Proloquo2Go, TouchChat, Avaz, TD Snap). — [App Store: Proloquo2Go](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id308368164); [App Store: TouchChat](https://apps.apple.com/app/id398860728); [Avaz support](https://avazapp.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/11000024972-in-what-languages-is-avaz-available-); [App Store: TD Snap](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/snap-core-first-aac/id1257753762)
- Indian-language options are limited to Avaz (paid; regional audio but English UI) and Jellow (free) among established tools. — [Ace Centre: Avaz India](https://acecentre.org.uk/language-library/avaz-india); [App Store India: Jellow](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/jellow-aac-communicator/id1456459072)
- Offline is a paywalled feature in Otsimo. — [App Store: Otsimo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/otsimo-special-education-1/id1084723774)
- Parents complain about subscription cost and mechanics across platforms (see section 5). — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/otsimo-special-education-aac); [App Store: Speech Blubs](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1239522573)
- DTT receptive-ID apps can teach labels efficiently: about 4.5 targets per hour in the Camp Discovery RCT, with skills maintained. This supports the 2-choice tap format, although generalisation was untested. — [PMC6411554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6411554)
- COPPA's 2025 amendments tighten rules on children's biometric data, and photos and voice of children are already personal information. — [Federal Register (govinfo)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm)

### Inferences
- **Personal-photo "who" trainer:** use the child's real family members (Mum, Dad, Grandma, self) in real contexts ("Who is eating?" showing a photo of Dad eating). No incumbent offers this, and own-photo stimuli may help generalisation compared with stock images. This is a hypothesis: the evidence base reviewed here did not test it.
- **Parent-recorded voice prompts in any language:** sidesteps TTS gaps in Marathi and Hindi and the English-only UI problem in Avaz India.
- **Two-choice field with errorless or prompting options:** matches DTT practice in Camp Discovery. Make it adjustable to 3–4 choices later.
- **Offline, on-device, no account, no ads, no subscription:** answers both the top parent complaint (cost and subscriptions) and the privacy and regulatory risk (COPPA, DPDP).
- **Maintained and simple:** the incumbents' review histories show abandonment (Camp Discovery, Leeloo), so reliability is a differentiator.
- **Extension path:** verb-based "Who is ___ing?" (action photos), then "Who has ___?" and "Who is happy?". The same data model (person × action photos) serves later "what/where" lessons, an area where Camp Discovery's "Actions" and "Locations" lessons exist only with generic images.
- **Parent co-play framing:** since generic apps "need an adult", building the app explicitly for parent-child joint use (e.g. the parent can trigger celebration or repeat the prompt) addresses the screen-time critique.
- **Positioning:** avoid therapy or efficacy claims. No autism therapeutic app is FDA-authorised, and incumbents' evidence claims draw scrutiny.

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly compares family-photo stimuli with stock photos or illustrations for WH or "who" learning in autistic children. This is likely covered by the clinical-guidance researcher, or remains an open question.
- No data was found on how many families currently improvise "who" practice with AAC apps or flashcard tools such as ABA Cards.
