How the data was gathered: App Store figures (price, average rating, number of ratings, last update, first release, version, languages, description) come from Apple's public iTunes Search/Lookup API for the US storefront, retrieved 26 Nov 2026. Each claim cites the matching App Store listing URL. Review quotes come from Apple's public customer-review RSS feed (`https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=<ID>/sortBy=mostRecent/json`). Google Play figures (rating, number of ratings, installs, "Updated on" date, ads/IAP flags) come from the public Play listing pages for the US/English store, fetched the same day. Ratings are US-storefront values. "n" is the number of ratings.

## Q1. Which apps specifically teach WH-questions or "who" questions, and which still exist and are maintained as of late 2026?

### Takeaway
About 20 dedicated WH-question apps are still listed in the US App Store, but most are iPad-era SLP tools that were last updated between 2015 and 2017. Only a handful were updated in 2025–2026: Smarty Ears WhQuestions, Teach Speech, "WH Questions: Why?", Happy Frog, and the new entrants Wh8 Kids and Question Clues. Android barely has any. Several apps that SLP lists used to recommend (QuestionIt, Rainbow Sentences, the Autism & PDD apps, the Kindergarten.com apps, Tactus "Question Therapy") no longer turn up in the US store.

- Search-API absence alone is weak evidence. A delisting is confirmed only for QuestionIt, where a lookup by ID returned nothing.

## Q2. App-by-app catalogue (developer, platform, price, audience, WH coverage, how "who" is taught, features)

### Takeaway
"Who" is almost always taught with generic stock content: illustrated or stock-photo occupations and anonymous people ("Who flies an airplane? …a pilot"; "Who is flying a kite?" with the choices boy/girl). Answers are given by tapping 2–4 choices or by speaking aloud while an adult scores. No dedicated WH app builds "who" around the child's own family photos. The closest is Hamaguchi's Fun with Who, What & Where, which has a "Who vs. What" discrimination activity, a selectable 2–4 choices, and one activity that puts the child's uploaded face photo into animations. It is iOS-only and has not been updated since 2020.

### Inferences
- In this category, "who" usually means the semantic class "a person or role" (pilot, firefighter, boy/girl), not "which familiar person is this". For a young autistic child learning "who" as a concept, that is a different and more abstract starting point than the parent's family-photo idea.
- The parent's planned design (own photos, spoken question, 2 picture choices, celebration) matches parts of what Hamaguchi, Grasshopper, Autism iHelp and Teach Speech already do: 2-choice taps, real photos, audio prompts, rewards. No single maintained, cross-platform app puts personal photos at the core of the "who" items.
- Smarty Ears and Super Duper are built for an SLP running sessions, with verbal answers, adult scoring and groups. They suit an SLP-led session more than independent play by a minimally verbal preschooler.

### Gaps
- For most apps, I could not confirm the exact number of answer choices, whether answer options are read aloud, or whether prompts can be re-recorded. Store listings rarely state this, and I did not have hands-on access.
- I could not open primary listings for Word SlapPs or the "i Get…" apps to confirm custom-photo and voice features or their current availability.
- An earlier search snippet claimed that Autism iHelp photos "can be replaced with custom photos". I could not verify it, and the Autism Parenting Magazine page itself does not say so. Treat it as unconfirmed.
- Hands-on depth of each app's "who" item set (how many "who" items, what kinds) is unknown beyond counts in descriptions (e.g., 56 per deck for Super Duper, 144 total for Autism iHelp SLP Edition).

## Q3. What do parents and SLPs say about what works and what doesn't?

### Takeaway
Reviewers value real photos or clear visuals, audio prompts for non-readers, the option to target one WH type at a time, and automatic data. Complaints cluster around abandonment and broken purchases (Super Duper WH Question Cards, Autism iHelp), poor distractors, robotic or monotone voices, reading demands, missing scaffolding, and rigid pacing. I found no Reddit threads with the tools available.

### Cited Findings

**What works**
- **Distinguishing question words, with cues faded:** an SLP used Happy Frog's WH Questions Skills with "LD and ASD kids… to distinguish between the types of WH questions", starting with written and pictured visuals and then "gradually fade all cues". — [App Store reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=1055252994/sortBy=mostRecent/json)
- **Teaching what each WH word means:** an SLP likes that Happy Frog's Answer & Ask "teaches what the various WH words mean" and "starts with a field of two and moves to a field of four". — [App Store reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=1055253477/sortBy=mostRecent/json)
- **Switching between question types:** SLPs say Smarty Ears helps children who "have difficulty switching between the different types of questions". They value visuals as cues and built-in data, mostly for ages 3–6. — [App Store reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=348905092/sortBy=mostRecent/json)

## Q4. Clear gaps and opportunities in the market

### Takeaway
No maintained app lets parents build "who" practice from their own family photos with their own recorded voice and a simple 2-choice tap. Nor does any app systematically teach who-vs-what-vs-where discrimination with errorless learning, prompt fading and mastery criteria, on both iOS and Android, affordably, and in languages beyond English and Spanish.

### Cited Findings
- **Personal-photo "who":**
  - None of the dedicated WH apps listed lets parents use their own photos as "who" items. The nearest is Hamaguchi's single face-upload animation activity (iOS, not updated since 2020). — [Hamaguchi Lite](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/who-what-and-where-lite/id1535316519)
  - Custom pictures and sounds exist only in general tools: Tactus ($89.99, aphasia-focused) and Bitsboard (DIY flashcards). — [Tactus](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/language-therapy-4-in-1/id525278822); [Bitsboard](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bitsboard-flashcards-games/id516842210)

### Inferences
- **Clearest open niche:** a parent-built, personal-photo "who" trainer. Its features would be:
  - family photos and the parent's recorded voice
  - errorless 2-choice tapping that grows to 3 or more choices
  - a who/what/where discrimination stage added later
  - simple mastery tracking
  - cross-platform, offline and ad-free
  
  No current competitor covers this combination. Hamaguchi proves the appeal of putting the child's own face into the app, but does it in only one activity, on iOS only, with no maintenance since 2020.
- **Low quality bar:** with ratings in the 2.5–4.3 range on small rating counts, a well-maintained app with reliable purchases, natural voices and logical distractors would stand out against the abandoned titles.
- **Indie entrants show demand but lack personalization:** the 2025–2026 entrants (Wh8 Kids, Question Clues) suggest renewed indie interest, but they use illustrated or text content, not personal photos.

### Gaps
- No usage or market-size data (downloads, revenue) is available for iOS apps. Google Play install bands were the only adoption signal (e.g., 10K+ for Olga Anisimova's app; 1K+ for Wh8 Kids).
- I did not check whether broad platforms (Otsimo, Speech Blubs, Proloquo2Go, Avaz) contain WH modules, which was out of scope. Otsimo and Speech Blubs did appear in Play results for "wh questions autism". — [Play search](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=wh+questions+autism&c=apps)
- Web-based options (e.g., Boom Cards WH decks with real photos) were not researched.
